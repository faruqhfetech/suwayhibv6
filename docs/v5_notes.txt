===============================================================================
ON NOVELTY -- WHERE IT ACTUALLY IS
===============================================================================

Asked directly whether v5 has novelty compared to v4. Honest answer: partly,
and not evenly distributed across the idea.

NOT NOVEL
  Teacher-student distillation. Standard technique. Nobody would call
  "distill Whisper into a smaller verifier" a new method on its own.

NOVEL: THE REASON FOR DISTILLING, NOT THE TECHNIQUE ITSELF
  Most distillation work assumes you WANT a smaller model and distills for
  speed/cost. Here the reason is different: v4a already showed, with a
  number, that six speakers cannot teach speaker invariance from scratch
  (holdout AP 0.064). Distillation isn't chosen for efficiency -- it's
  chosen because there is no other way to get invariance into a small model
  given the data available. That's a specific, evidenced rationale, not a
  default choice.

  *** REVISED AFTER THE ABLATION -- see "THE ABLATION" section below. ***
  The rationale above was the MOTIVATION for choosing distillation, and it
  remains an honest account of why the choice was made. But it is NOT what
  the teacher turned out to contribute. The ablation shows both models
  transfer to unseen reciters comparably; what distillation supplies is an
  ABSOLUTE SCALE (calibration), because the metric loss only constrains
  relative ordering and CTC only sees positives, so nothing else anchors
  what a score should mean.

  The claim to make is therefore: "distillation from a well-generalising
  teacher supplies CALIBRATION that relative objectives cannot". NOT
  "distillation transfers speaker invariance" (the ablation killed that),
  and NOT "worth ~0.10 correlation" as a capability claim (the threshold
  sweep showed that gap was calibration; on balanced accuracy the
  no-distill model is marginally better at 81.8% vs 79.9%).

  What survives is narrow and deployment-oriented: the distilled model
  produces a score that MEANS something at a natural threshold, which the
  purely relative objectives cannot produce. That is worth having for a
  device. It is not a better model.

NOVEL IF IT WORKS: THE REFRAMING, NOT THE MODEL SIZE
  "Does the expected text appear in this audio", combined with the strictly
  sequential wait-and-restart pipeline (section 5 of project_plan.txt), is a
  genuine systems decomposition. The Qur'an CAPT literature is GOP-and-
  forced-alignment almost universally. Nobody there frames it as streaming
  detection with a stall-and-repeat recovery loop.

  THE ACTUAL THESIS, sharpened after the HMM conversation below:
  the closed-vocabulary streaming reframing is the right abstraction for
  this task, and MULTIPLE MODEL CLASSES can fill it in. Whisper forced-
  decoding fills it in expensively (v4). A distilled verifier fills it in
  cheaply (v5, hoped). An HMM might fill it in almost for free (untested).
  That is a better thesis for a systems-design course than "here is a
  smaller model" -- it is "here is the right way to decompose the problem,
  and here is what happens when different components are plugged into that
  decomposition."

NOVEL EITHER WAY: THE COMPARISON ITSELF
  A matched-accuracy, matched-corpus comparison between a general
  fine-tuned ASR decoder and a small distilled verifier, on the same
  held-out speakers, is useful regardless of which wins. Nobody has
  published that specific comparison for this task.

WHAT WOULD MAKE IT FEEL THIN
  If the held-out-reciter check (project_plan.txt section 9, step 3) fails
  and the project pushes forward without engaging the failure. The result
  becomes "we tried something, it didn't clearly work, here's a system
  anyway" -- coursework, but it loses what made v3->v4 compelling: a
  DIAGNOSED result, not just an attempted one.


===============================================================================
THE HMM / SMALL-MODEL PRIOR  -- worth taking seriously, not a digression
===============================================================================

Raised in passing ("even an HMM can be useful enough for this") and it is
actually load-bearing, not a tangent.

THE ARGUMENT
  An HMM, or a small CTC/keyword-spotting model, is BUILT for exactly this
  task: bounded vocabulary, known sequence, "does this state sequence
  explain this audio." That is the classical formulation of keyword
  spotting and forced alignment from before end-to-end seq2seq models took
  over. It is a much better structural prior for this task than a general
  seq2seq decoder (which is what whisper-base-quran is).

  If a simple model with strong inductive bias for closed-vocabulary
  sequential matching does the job, that is evidence FOR the closed-
  vocabulary reframing, not against the neural student. It says the task
  does not need a large model's flexibility -- it needs the right
  structural assumptions matched to the problem.

PROPOSED USE: A CHEAP BASELINE, TRIED BEFORE THE NEURAL STUDENT
  A small acoustic model (GMM-HMM style, or a tiny CTC model) explicitly
  modelling "expected word sequence, allow skips, allow repeats, score
  frame-by-frame fit."

  If it gets most of the way there, the 15M distilled verifier has to beat
  THAT, not just beat nothing -- a much more honest bar.

  If the HMM alone is competitive, that is a genuinely interesting finding
  in its own right: the task was solvable with much older technology once
  framed correctly, and the interesting content of v5 is the PIPELINE (the
  closed-vocabulary decomposition, the sequential wait-and-restart
  recovery), not the model size.

STATUS: not yet decided whether this is a parallel track or stays as a
"worth remembering" note. Revisit before committing engineering time to the
neural student -- it may be the cheaper first experiment, and a fast
negative or positive result from it changes how much distillation work is
actually justified.


===============================================================================
TEACHER TARGET EXTRACTION -- RESULTS, AND THE madd_long QUESTION
===============================================================================

Ran extract at n=4000 (train), n=4000 (val, seed 2), n=1000 (holdout, the
two held-out reciters). Positive rate stable at ~12% across all three --
train/val/holdout are comparable samples.

FOUR OF SIX CORRUPTION TYPES: CLEAN, STABLE, CONFIRMED ON HOLDOUT
  deletion, duration_match, random_word, same_ayah all sit at median 0.0000
  with p90 under ~0.002 in EVERY split, including holdout. The teacher is
  not fooled by same-voice, matched-duration substitutions even on unseen
  reciters. Strong, learnable signal to distill from.

  IMPORTANT DISTINCTION, stated explicitly because it is easy to conflate:
  this confirms the TEACHER discriminates well on synthetic corruptions. It
  does NOT confirm distillation works -- no student has been trained yet.
  The actual test is whether a small student trained on this data
  reproduces the discrimination on targets_holdout.jsonl. That is the
  project_plan.txt step 3 gate, still unrun.

madd_long IS BROKEN, NOT NOISY
  Median target on the "stretched word" case: 0.40 (train, n=230), 0.14
  (val, n=232), 0.82 (holdout, n=68). High and inconsistent across every
  split, WORSE on holdout. This is not the n=4 pilot's small-sample noise --
  it reproduces at real sample sizes.

  WORKING HYPOTHESIS: stretching a word 2-4x may just sound like a slow,
  deliberate reciter to the teacher, not an error. The reference reciters
  already vary hugely in tempo (Husary is notably slow), so "read this word
  very slowly" and "elongated this word incorrectly" may be acoustically
  close from the model's point of view.

  SUPPORTING EVIDENCE FROM v3: v3's madd_lengthening recall was ALSO only
  0.380 (project_plan.txt / current_v3.txt Part 3). Two different systems,
  same weak spot. That is either a real property of the task (pace-based
  elongation errors are just hard to catch by acoustic modelling alone) or
  a shared blind spot in how both systems were built to look for it.

madd_short IS WEAKER BUT USABLE
  Median near 0, but wide p90 (0.33-0.99 across splits) -- a meaningful
  fraction of examples do not drop. Possibly the same "sounds like a fast
  reciter" explanation, inverted.

DECISION POINT -- NOT YET MADE, RETURN TO THIS BEFORE FINALISING THE
TRAINING MIX:
  do we FIX madd_long (reweight it down in the training loss, or push the
  stretch factor to something unambiguous so the teacher is forced to drop
  its score), OR do we ACCEPT that pace-based elongation detection is weak
  by nature -- matching v3's own finding -- and let the student inherit that
  same known limitation rather than over-engineering around a case that may
  not be fixable with this teacher at all.

  Leaning note (not a decision): if v3 independently found the same
  weakness with a completely different method, that argues for "accept and
  document" over "fight it" -- but worth testing whether a more extreme
  stretch factor changes the picture before concluding that.


===============================================================================
RESULTS -- THE STUDENT, AND WHY THE FIRST ARCHITECTURE WAS WRONG
===============================================================================

HEADLINE (holdout = Husary + Minshawy, never seen in training)

    architecture                      params   holdout r   corrupt<0.1
    BCE on a scalar, width 256          1.0M      0.561        32%
    BCE on a scalar, width 512          3.8M      0.618        44%
    CTC + metric + distillation         811K      0.690        67%

  These are PEARSON r on the embedding head only, which is how the runs
  were originally scored. The final result uses BOTH heads and is measured
  by balanced accuracy and AUC instead -- 86.7% / 0.919 on holdout. See
  "THE CTC HEAD" below. Pearson r is kept here only for continuity with the
  earlier runs; it is not the metric to report.

  Better on every axis at FEWER parameters than either earlier attempt.
  Capacity was never the constraint; the training objective was.

THE GATE PASSED
  Val r 0.732 vs holdout r 0.690 on two reciters the student never heard.
  This is the question v5 rested on, and it is the thing that FAILED in v4a
  (val AP 0.54, holdout AP 0.064). Distillation transferred the teacher's
  speaker invariance.

WHY THE REDESIGN WORKED -- the loss curves say it directly
  ctc     31.4 -> 0.97     frame-level alignment supervision
  metric   3.44 -> 0.08    started at ~ln(64)=4.16, i.e. chance for a
                           64-way discrimination, so it did real work
  distill  0.694 -> 0.105

  Rewrite 1 had ONE scalar doing all three jobs: teaching what a correct
  word sounds like, teaching alignment implicitly, and teaching the
  accept/reject decision. Splitting them was the whole difference.

  Adopted from Jin et al., Interspeech 2024 ("CTC-aligned Audio-Text
  Embedding for Streaming Open-vocabulary Keyword Spotting"), whose
  155K-parameter acoustic encoder reaches competitive accuracy -- which is
  what settled the capacity question before we ran another width sweep:
    1. CTC loss over characters (alignment supervision)
    2. cosine similarity instead of a learned MLP head
    3. in-batch negatives (~B-1 per positive, vs ~0.35 from mismatch
       sampling)
    4. word-level pooling, not per-character -- their ablation found
       character-level the WEAKEST of three levels tried

  Kept, against the literature: DISTILLATION, as a third loss term. Six
  reciters cannot teach speaker invariance; CTC and metric learning would
  have trained it from OUR six speakers. See the ablation below.

THE ANOMALY THAT TURNED OUT TO BE A BUG
  `clean>0.5` sat pinned at 74-80% across EVERY configuration -- different
  data, widths, learning rates, epoch counts. A metric that refuses to move
  while everything around it changes is usually structural, not a tuning
  issue. It was: MAX_CHARS was 12, which truncated six of eleven common
  Qur'anic words, and the lost characters are word-final diacritics --
  exactly the kasra/sukun/tanwin contrasts the system exists to detect.
  After 12 -> 20, clean>0.5 reached 84-97%.

SATURATION
  Val r plateaus at ~0.73 from epoch 21; epochs 30-35 are flat at 0.729
  while the losses keep falling. More epochs will not help. The remaining
  gains are in the data issues below, not in training longer.


===============================================================================
KNOWN DATA ISSUES -- NOT YET FIXED
===============================================================================

  SPLIT SIZES: NON-STANDARD BUT NOT A DEFECT. 16,453 train / 16,300 val is
  a 50/50 split. Originally flagged here as wrong and recommended for
  regeneration at 20k/4k/4k -- THAT RECOMMENDATION IS WITHDRAWN. It
  conflated size with overlap. The size costs some epoch time; it does not
  bias anything, and a larger val gives a lower-variance AUC estimate,
  which matters now that AUC drives checkpoint selection. See the
  CONFIGURATION section.

  TRAIN AND VAL OVERLAP. Both were drawn from the SAME pool (six reciters,
  557 ayahs), differing only by random seed, sampling 4000 variants from
  3318 ayah-reciter pairs WITH REPLACEMENT. Each pair appears in a split
  with probability 1 - e^(-4000/3318) = 70%, so ~49% of pairs (about 1628)
  are in BOTH. For half the val set the model has already seen the same
  reciter saying the same ayah -- only the augmentation and corruption
  differ.

    Consequence: val r 0.732 is inflated, and since checkpoints are now
    selected on val r, selection partly rewards memorisation. The holdout
    gap (0.732 vs 0.690) is about the size that contamination predicts.

    HOLDOUT IS STILL CLEAN -- different reciters entirely -- so 0.690
    stands as the honest number.

    Fix: partition the key list in teacher.py BEFORE drawing, splitting by
    (reciter, ayah) pair. That matches deployment: closed vocabulary, novel
    audio. v4's pairs.py did split by ayah deliberately; that discipline
    was lost in v5.


===============================================================================
THE ABLATION -- RUN, AND THE PREDICTION WAS WRONG
===============================================================================

    python student.py train ... --w-distill 0 --out runs/student_nodistill

RESULT
                          val r    holdout r    gap (val - holdout)
    with distillation     0.732      0.690           +0.042
    without               0.542      0.592           -0.050

    distillation is worth ~0.10 holdout correlation
    *** but see THRESHOLD SWEEP below: that 0.10 is CALIBRATION, not
    discrimination. On balanced accuracy the no-distill model is
    slightly BETTER. ***

WHAT WAS PREDICTED, WRITTEN BEFORE THE RUN
  "holdout r should drop MORE than val r. Val shares reciters with train,
  so a student trained without the teacher can still do well there by
  learning those six voices. Holdout is the only place the invariance
  matters."

WHAT ACTUALLY HAPPENED
  The opposite. Removing distillation cost val 0.190 and holdout only
  0.098. The no-distill model scores HIGHER on holdout than on val -- an
  inverted gap.

  So distillation is NOT primarily supplying speaker invariance. Both
  models transfer to unseen reciters comparably; the with-distillation
  model is simply better everywhere, and disproportionately better on the
  contaminated val split.

WHAT IT IS ACTUALLY SUPPLYING: AN ABSOLUTE SCALE
  Without the teacher term, `clean>0.5` pins at 99.3-99.9% for 34 straight
  epochs while `corrupt<0.1` crawls to 11-15%. Every output drifts high.

  The mechanism is structural, not incidental:
    - the metric loss only constrains RELATIVE ordering within a batch
    - CTC only ever sees POSITIVE examples (mismatched pairs are excluded,
      since aligning characters that are not there is meaningless)
    - so NOTHING in the remaining objective anchors what an absolute score
      should mean

  The distillation term is the only signal that says "this word should
  score 0.97 and that one 0.003". That is calibration, and it turns out to
  be what the teacher was mostly contributing.

  Supporting observation, though NOT proof: the `distill` column rises
  0.703 -> 1.696 while unoptimised, i.e. the other two objectives actively
  move the model away from the teacher's scale. Consistent with the
  calibration reading; also consistent with the two models simply being
  different, so it should not be leaned on.

STILL TO CHECK -- SPEARMAN vs PEARSON
  *** ANSWERED. See "THRESHOLD SWEEP" below -- and the answer overturns
  this section's conclusion. ***
  A threshold sweep showed the no-distill model reaching 81.8% balanced
  accuracy against 79.9% for the distilled one. Its worse Pearson r came
  from compressed outputs, not worse discrimination. Distillation buys
  CALIBRATION, at no capability gain and possibly a small cost.

THE INVERTED GAP IS EVIDENCE FOR THE VAL CONTAMINATION
  Val overlaps train on ~49% of ayah-reciter pairs. The with-distillation
  model can exploit that (val 0.732 > holdout 0.690). The no-distill model,
  whose objectives are purely relative, apparently cannot (val 0.542 <
  holdout 0.592). That asymmetry is itself a symptom of the split problem
  documented above, and another reason to fix it.

WHY THE FAILED PREDICTION IS WORTH KEEPING
  It was written down before the run, so it could be wrong. A prediction
  quietly revised afterwards would have produced a tidier story and a less
  trustworthy one. The finding -- that the teacher's contribution here is
  calibration rather than invariance -- is more interesting than the one
  expected, and it changes what the v5 framing should claim.

  NOTE ON THE EARLIER READING OF THIS RUN: at epoch 13 this was called
  "collapsed to saying yes to everything". That was wrong and was corrected
  -- a constant output would have r near zero, and r was 0.53 and rising.
  The outputs are shifted high while the ordering underneath is informative.
  That is a threshold-position observation, not a collapse. Reading
  clean>0.5 / corrupt<0.1 as performance measures is a trap that caught us
  repeatedly.

THRESHOLD SWEEP -- SUPERSEDED, SEE "THE CTC HEAD" BELOW
===============================================================================
*** The conclusion in this section ("no-distill is better") is WRONG. It
*** used only the embedding head and an unfair comparison. Kept because the
*** per-model threshold tables and the Pearson-vs-calibration lesson are
*** still valid. The model-vs-model verdict is not.

Both models, holdout, sweeping the decision threshold instead of reading a
fixed one.

  WITHOUT distillation          positives median 0.998, p05 0.952
                                negatives median 0.767, p95 0.999
      thr    keep pos   catch neg   balanced
      0.50     99.7%      36.9%      68.3%
      0.75     99.3%      49.1%      74.2%
      0.90     98.2%      59.9%      79.1%
      0.95     95.4%      66.2%      80.8%
      0.98     89.5%      74.1%      81.8%   <-- best
      0.99     82.2%      79.5%      80.9%

  WITH distillation             positives median 0.763, p05 0.086
                                negatives median 0.030, p95 0.832
      thr    keep pos   catch neg   balanced
      0.50     73.4%      86.4%      79.9%   <-- best
      0.75     51.3%      92.7%      72.0%
      0.90     29.2%      97.3%      63.3%

THE RESULT  *** LATER SHOWN WRONG -- see "THE CTC HEAD" ***
  Best balanced accuracy: 81.8% WITHOUT distillation, 79.9% WITH.
  This used only the EMBEDDING head. With both heads the ordering reverses:
  86.7% with distillation vs 85.1% without.

  The no-distill model is SLIGHTLY BETTER at the actual discrimination once
  the threshold is allowed to move. Its Pearson r was worse purely because
  its outputs are compressed into a high, narrow band -- which Pearson
  penalises and a threshold sweep does not care about.

SO THE CLAIM SHRINKS AGAIN  *** and then partly un-shrinks, see below ***
  not  "distillation transfers speaker invariance"      (ablation killed it)
  not  "distillation is worth ~0.10 correlation"        (that was measuring
                                                         calibration)
  but  "distillation produces a CALIBRATED score at no cost -- arguably a
        marginal cost -- to discrimination"
  The "no cost to discrimination" part did not survive: with both heads
  consulted, distillation is worth ~1.5pp balanced accuracy as well.

  That is a deployment convenience, not a capability gain. Useful: it means
  the device can use a natural threshold near 0.5 and the score means
  something. But it is not a better model.

THE METRIC LESSON, AGAIN, AND THIS TIME IT WAS PEARSON
  Correlation was insisted on all evening as "the honest, threshold-free
  metric" -- and it misled in exactly the way the threshold rates were
  accused of. Pearson is threshold-free but NOT calibration-free: it
  penalises a model whose ranking is fine but whose outputs are squashed.

  AUC is the right comparison between two models on different scales:
  rank-based, so threshold-free AND calibration-free. It should have been
  used from the start for any model-vs-model claim.

  Three metrics have now each been trusted too far in turn:
    corrupt<0.1  inflated by growing overconfidence
    device top-1 an UNTRAINED model scored 0.993 on it (v4)
    Pearson r    penalises compression, not just error
  The pattern is not "use metric X". It is: cross-check any headline number
  against a metric that fails differently, before believing it.

PRACTICAL CONSEQUENCE FOR THE DEVICE
  At thr 0.95: keep 95.4% of correct recitations, catch 66.2% of errors.
  At thr 0.98: keep 89.5%, catch 74.1%.
  Given the design principle carried from v3 -- a false stall costs one
  repetition, a false confirmation teaches something wrong -- 0.95 is
  probably the right operating point, not the balanced-accuracy optimum.


===============================================================================
THE CTC HEAD -- BEST RESULT OF THE PROJECT, AND IT WAS SITTING UNUSED
===============================================================================

The CTC head was TRAINED but never CONSULTED at inference. The reference
system (Jin et al.) combines both heads:  z = z_CTC + lambda * z_embed.
Tested on holdout, both models, sweeping lambda.

                          with distill        no distill
    embedding alone     82.7% / AUC 0.895   82.0% / AUC 0.880
    CTC alone           84.9% / AUC 0.905   83.2% / AUC 0.890
    COMBINED            86.7% / AUC 0.919   85.1% / AUC 0.905
    best lambda              2.0                 0.5

FINDING 1 -- THE CTC HEAD IS THE STRONGER ONE
  84.9% vs 82.7% in the distilled model; 83.2% vs 82.0% without. The head
  that was never used at inference beats the one that was, in BOTH models.
  This is the largest single result here and it cost nothing to obtain --
  the head was already trained.

FINDING 2 -- THE TWO HEADS ARE COMPLEMENTARY
  Combined beats both alone, and improves monotonically with lambda up to
  ~2.0 before turning over. The reference paper's inference formula
  reproduces on this data.

FINDING 3 -- lambda IS MODEL-SPECIFIC, NOT A CONSTANT
  2.0 for the distilled model, 0.5 for the ablation. Must be tuned per
  checkpoint on a dev split.

*** THIS REVERSES THE THRESHOLD-SWEEP CONCLUSION ABOVE ***
  That section concluded the no-distill model was BETTER (81.8% vs 79.9%).
  Wrong, for two reasons: the sweep script used only the EMBEDDING head,
  and the two models' sweeps were not run on comparable footing.

  With both heads and a fair comparison, DISTILLATION WINS ON EVERY
  MEASURE, including AUC -- which is rank-based, so this is NOT
  calibration. It is a real discrimination gain of ~1.5 points balanced
  accuracy and ~0.014 AUC.

WHERE THE CLAIM FINALLY LANDS
  not  "distillation transfers speaker invariance"   -- ablation killed it,
                                                        and that still holds
  not  "the no-distill model is better"              -- measurement artifact
  but  "distillation gives a modest discrimination gain (~1.5pp balanced,
        ~0.014 AUC) AND a calibrated score, at 811K parameters"

HEADLINE NUMBER FOR v5
  86.7% balanced accuracy, AUC 0.919, on HELD-OUT RECITERS, from an
  811K-parameter model combining both heads at lambda 2.0.

FOUR REVERSALS IN ONE EVENING, ALL FROM BETTER MEASUREMENT
    1. "distillation transfers invariance"     -> ablation: no
    2. "worth ~0.10 correlation"               -> sweep: that was calibration
    3. "no-distill is actually better"         -> both heads: no, it is worse
    4. "correlation is the honest metric"      -> AUC: Pearson penalises
                                                  compression, not error
  None of these came from changing the models. All four came from measuring
  the same models more carefully. The models were fine; the measurements
  were not.

CONFIGURATION -- WHAT EACH RUN ACTUALLY USED
===============================================================================

Recorded because the defaults changed AFTER the results above were
measured. Any number quoted earlier belongs to the BASELINE column.

                          BASELINE (runs 1-2)      CURRENT (after changes)
  batch                          64                        256
  epochs                         35                        100
  lr                           3e-4                       3e-4
  width / blocks              192 / 6                    192 / 6
  params                      810,544                    810,544
  w_distill / w_metric / w_ctc  1 / 1 / 0.3               1 / 1 / 0.3
  mismatch_p                    0.35                       0.35
  MAX_CHARS                       20                         20
  inference head            EMBEDDING ONLY           both (lambda 2.0)
  checkpoint selection      Pearson r on val         AUC on val
  train / val / holdout      16453 / 16300 / 4009      unchanged

  So the 86.7% / AUC 0.919 headline was obtained from a checkpoint that was
  SELECTED on Pearson r and evaluated post-hoc with both heads. A model
  selected on AUC from the start has not yet been trained.

WHY EACH DEFAULT CHANGED

  batch 64 -> 256
    The metric loss fell to 0.034 by epoch 35 -- with 63 in-batch negatives
    the discrimination was solved and had stopped producing gradient.
    Literature: small batches make InfoNCE approach zero "at the same
    magnitude with the floating-point error... it may seriously affect the
    training". The reference system uses 1024.
    Caveat: one paper reports the OPPOSITE (fewer negatives helping), so
    this is contested in general -- but the loss hitting 0.034 is direct
    evidence that OURS had gone uninformative.

  epochs 35 -> 100
    OneCycleLR annealed lr to 4.9e-6 by epoch 33 and 3.0e-8 by epoch 35.
    The "plateau" at epochs 30-35 was the SCHEDULE, not convergence. This
    was called saturation earlier and that was wrong. The reference system
    trains 100 epochs.

  inference: embedding only -> both heads
    The CTC head was trained and never consulted. It is the STRONGER head
    (84.9% vs 82.7% balanced) and the combination beats both (86.7%).

  selection: Pearson r -> AUC
    r penalises compressed outputs even when the ordering is correct. The
    ablation looked 0.10 worse by r and was only ~1.5pp worse by balanced
    accuracy. AUC is rank-based, so it measures what survives thresholding.

WHAT WAS *NOT* CHANGED, AND WHY

  THE SPLIT SIZES. 16453 / 16300 / 4009 is a 50/50 train/val split, which
  is non-standard but not wrong. Two reasons to keep it:
    - the real defect is the OVERLAP (~49% of ayah-reciter pairs appear in
      both train and val), not the size. Shrinking val would not fix that.
    - a larger val set gives a LOWER-VARIANCE estimate, which matters more
      now that checkpoint selection runs on AUC, a noisier statistic than
      a loss.
  An earlier note in this file conflated size with overlap and recommended
  regenerating at 5000/1000/1000. That recommendation is withdrawn; the
  overlap fix (partitioning keys before drawing) stands on its own.


===============================================================================
PROCESS NOTE -- WHERE THE TIME ACTUALLY WENT
===============================================================================

Six training runs to the first trustworthy result. Three were wasted, and
every one of the three came from a SILENT failure, not a crash:

  1. neg_weight left at 4.0 after mismatch negatives changed the class
     balance from 12% to 49% positives -- negatives ended up carrying 81%
     of the loss and the model collapsed to predicting ~0 everywhere.
  2. --max-examples silently ignored when --cache-train was used, so the
     "500-example overfit test" was never an overfit test. Two runs were
     byte-identical and it took comparing them line by line to notice.
  3. render() changed to return three values; one of its two call sites was
     not updated. A bare `except: continue` inside an uncapped while-loop
     turned a one-line signature mismatch into a 30-minute stall with zero
     output.

Also silent, and only found by reading the file end to end:
  - a stateful RNG meant the val set was REDRAWN EVERY EPOCH, so the val
    curve was measured on different data each pass -- and forked DataLoader
    workers all shared the same draw sequence
  - checkpoint selection on val loss saved epoch 17 while correlation kept
    improving to 35
  - word spans recomputed from pre-corruption timings, putting the window
    on the wrong audio for every word after a corruption (~18% of examples
    carrying ~1.0 targets)

  THE LESSON: fail loudly, and assert assumptions in code rather than in
  your head. `assert len(tr) == expected` would have caught (2) in seconds.
  Every one of these produced plausible-looking output that cost a run to
  diagnose.

A METRIC LESSON WORTH KEEPING
  An earlier checkpoint scored corrupt<0.1 = 62.6% with r = 0.271; a better
  one scored 32% with r = 0.510. The rate was being inflated by growing
  OVERCONFIDENCE, not better discrimination. Threshold-based rates move
  when a model's output distribution shifts; correlation does not. This is
  the same failure as the v4 device-localization metric on which an
  UNTRAINED model scored 0.993.

  Caveat recorded honestly: "val loss rising means the metric is being
  gamed" is TOO STRONG as a general claim, and was pushed back on
  correctly. Cross-entropy punishes confident errors disproportionately, so
  a model can sharpen its outputs -- worsening loss -- while ranking
  improves. The width-512 run is the clean counterexample: val loss rose
  from 0.506 to 0.70 while holdout correlation reached its best value yet.
  Divergence is a REASON TO CHECK, not proof.
