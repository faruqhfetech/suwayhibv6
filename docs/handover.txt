SUWAYHIB -- HANDOVER
Written to start a fresh conversation. Everything needed to continue is
here; nothing assumes you saw the earlier work.


===============================================================================
0. THE PROJECT
===============================================================================

Learning to recite the Qur'an correctly needs a teacher's ear -- someone who
catches a mispronounced word, a skipped elongation, a merged sound. Between
lessons learners are on their own, and small mistakes become habits. This is
sharpest in under-resourced settings like Qur'an schools in Kaduna, where
students practise independently and teacher time is scarce.

Existing tools need an internet connection and send the recitation to a
cloud server. That is a barrier where connectivity is unreliable, and a
discomfort for families who would rather a private religious practice not
leave the room.

Suwayhib is a small, low-cost, standalone practice companion that runs
entirely offline. Nothing is uploaded, nothing needs signal, nothing about
the learner's voice leaves the room.

THE GOAL NOW: A STREAMING SYSTEM.
Not record-then-submit. The learner picks a starting verse and simply
recites. Words confirm on screen as they are spoken. This is the project;
it is not negotiable down to batch processing.


===============================================================================
1. WHAT STREAMING MEANS HERE
===============================================================================

THE USER EXPERIENCE
  - the learner selects a starting verse and presses start ONCE
  - they recite continuously; no button between verses
  - the verse text is displayed, and words turn GREEN as they are confirmed
  - confirmation is MONOTONIC and never retracted -- a word that goes green
    and then un-greens is worse than no feedback at all
  - if the green edge stops advancing, they have hit something. They can
    keep going, or tap any word to reset the cursor there ("from here")
  - the session ends on a stop press or ~4 seconds of silence

  NO RED. A wrong word is not marked. The green edge simply stops. This is
  deliberate and carried from v3/v4: a false stall costs the learner one
  repetition, a false accusation costs trust -- and this is a child being
  told they got the Qur'an wrong. Information without accusation.

THE TECHNICAL SHAPE
  - browser captures with MediaRecorder on a ~250ms timeslice, streams
    chunks over a WebSocket
  - the server holds session state: audio buffer, cursor position, confirmed
    words
  - on each arriving chunk:

        while true:
            w = expected_words[cursor]
            found, end = detect(w, audio, from=audio_pos)
            if found:
                confirm(w); cursor += 1; audio_pos = end
            else:
                break        # wait for more audio

  - STRICTLY SEQUENTIAL, NO LOOKAHEAD. Wait for word k before considering
    k+1. This costs the ability to spot a later error while an earlier one
    is unresolved, but removes cascade failure entirely -- the system can
    never drift out of alignment because it never advances past something
    unresolved.
  - one chunk may confirm SEVERAL words if the learner is ahead; the cursor
    catches up greedily
  - on a stall: after a timeout, pause and ask the learner to take it again
    from that word. The system does NOT have to work out WHY the word is
    missing (skipped, mispronounced, still coming, stopped) -- only that it
    is stuck. That collapses four hard classification problems into one easy
    one.

THE CORE QUESTION THE MODEL MUST ANSWER
  Given a chunk of audio and an expected word: DOES THIS WORD APPEAR IN THIS
  AUDIO, and if so where does it end?

  Note this is DETECTION, not verification. It must be able to say NOT
  FOUND. That distinction is the single most important thing in this
  document -- see section 4.


===============================================================================
2. WHAT ALREADY WORKS  (v1 - v4)
===============================================================================

v1  MFCC/DTW FEATURES + CLASSIFIER SHOPPING
    82 AUC, F1 ~60. Swapping MLP, logistic regression and tree models over
    the same features moved it a couple of points each. Three model families
    with very different inductive biases all capping out is the signature of
    a REPRESENTATION limit, not a model limit.

v3  DURATION DEVIATION -- no training, no model file
    Aligns the learner against reference recitations by OTHER reciters with
    DTW, maps known word boundaries through the warp path, and scores each
    word on how far its implied duration sits from what the reference
    reciters agree on.

      detection AUPRC 0.901 (baseline 0.700), holdout 0.915
      artifact-control gap -0.007, top-1 localization 0.543
      truncation 0.944  deletion 0.758  repetition 0.344
      madd 0.380-0.388  substitution 0.360  near-miss 0.250

    Best single feature (mean_log_dev) reached 0.918 holdout AP alone vs
    0.925 for logistic regression over seven features. Seven thousandths was
    not worth a training dependency on a Pi, so v3 ships the single feature.

    STRONG on structure, BLIND on segmental content -- duration cannot see a
    wrong phoneme of the right length.

v4a CONTRASTIVE ACOUSTIC WORD EMBEDDINGS -- implemented, ABANDONED
    val AP 0.54 (unseen words, seen voices) but holdout AP 0.064 (unseen
    VOICES). It learned words, not speaker invariance.

    THE REASON, and it is the transferable finding: speaker-invariance work
    in the literature trains on HUNDREDS TO THOUSANDS of speakers. This
    corpus has SIX. With six voices there are not enough directions of
    speaker variation to separate "voice" from "word", so the model encodes
    voice -- because with six voices, voice IS a reliable cue. No
    architecture or loss change fixes a data limit.

v4b FORCED DECODING -- THIS IS THE SHIPPED DEVICE, AND IT WORKS
    The verse is known before the learner recites. So teacher-force a
    Whisper decoder with that text and read the probability it assigns to
    each token. No training, no gallery, no per-word threshold.

    Model: tarteel-ai/whisper-base-ar-quran (72.6M). Fine-tuned on Qur'anic
    Arabic by Tarteel, whose training data is crowdsourced recitations from
    THOUSANDS of ordinary users -- exactly the speaker diversity this corpus
    lacks, already in the weights.

    MEASURED, worst-token probability per word:
      reference reciter, 160 words   median 1.0000, p05 0.9993, min 0.9913
      learner, correct, careful      0.51 - 1.00 (10 verses, no false flags)
      learner, deliberate س -> ص     0.000
      wrong verse entirely           0.000 (every word)
    Nothing observed between 0.13 and 0.51. The threshold needed no fitting.

    DEPLOYMENT: ~300 MB, 0.48s per attempt on laptop CPU (RTF 0.08),
    estimated 2-2.5s on a Pi 4. Forced decoding is a SINGLE forward pass --
    the tokens are known, so no autoregressive generation and no beam
    search. That is why it is cheaper than transcription.

    A WORKED EXAMPLE. 78:9 نَوْمَكُمْ scored 0.000 across three takes. We
    suspected a model defect -- the reference reciter scores 0.982 on the
    same word and text. Reciting it more deliberately moved it to 0.13, then
    0.99. The model had been right every time; the articulation was weak.

    LIMITATION: v4 is RECORD-THEN-SUBMIT. It scores a whole verse after the
    learner finishes. It is not streaming, which is why v5 exists.

v5  WHERE IT STANDS -- see section 4

manifest_full.csv
  18,144 VERIFIED word segments:
      reciter, surah, ayah, word_idx, start_ms, end_ms,
      multiword_segment, audio_path

  557 of 564 ayahs survived verification. Checks that produced it: monotonic
  segments, segments inside the audio, word-count agreement ACROSS reciters,
  word-index coverage, implausibly short words, cross-reciter duration
  outliers.

  THE CHECK THAT EARNED ITS KEEP: rows must divide evenly by reciter count.
  Eight reciters saying identical text cannot produce a fractional row
  count. That one assertion caught two parse bugs that passed every other
  structural test -- an exclusive-vs-inclusive index error, and reciters
  with SKIPPED word indices.

  multiword_segment=1 marks words the aligner could not split apart. Only 2
  in the whole corpus. Those words cannot be isolated for playback.

manifest_full_suspects.csv
  570 words whose duration disagrees with the other reciters. A TRIAGE
  QUEUE, not a defect list -- forced alignment always has a tail.

recs/
  SEVEN LEARNER RECORDINGS, one adult male voice, with descriptions.txt:

      Recording.m4a    | 78 6  | correct
      Recording_1.m4a  | 78 7  | correct
      Recording_2.m4a  | 78 8  | correct
      Recording_3.m4a  | 78 9  | correct
      Recording_4.m4a  | 78 10 | error - wrong word (a DIFFERENT verse)
      Recording_5.m4a  | 78 9  | correct
      Recording_6.m4a  | 78 9  | error - wrong letter (س/ص)

  THESE ARE THE ONLY UNCONTAMINATED EVALUATION DATA IN THE PROJECT. The
  reference reciters are almost certainly inside Whisper-Quran's
  fine-tuning set (Tarteel trained on EveryAyah, the same corpus), so
  held-out-reciter numbers are an upper bound. This voice is not.

  Recording_5 and Recording_6 are the key pair: same verse, same voice,
  minutes apart, one correct and one with a deliberate س -> ص swap. Under
  v4's forced decoding they score 0.994 and 0.000 on that word.

texts/
  Six Tanzil script variants, 6236 lines each. THREE RULES, all derived by
  measurement (scoring a professional reciter, who is correct by
  construction, against each variant):

    1. USE quran-simple-plain. Mean worst-token by variant:
         simple-plain 0.87, simple 0.65, uthmani 0.25, the -min variants
         0.00. The Tarteel models were fine-tuned on imlaei script.
    2. STRIP U+0670 (SUPERSCRIPT ALEF). This ONE codepoint caused 9 of 11
       mismatched ayahs -- every alif-maqsura ending (مُوسَىٰ, إِلَىٰ) and
       الرَّحْمَٰن. Removing it took the reciter from 11/60 failures to 2/60.
    3. STRIP THE BASMALA from ayah 1 of every surah except 1 and 9. Tanzil
       prepends it; the audio does not contain it. Take the prefix FROM THE
       TEXT -- verse 1:1 IS the basmala. Two hand-typed attempts failed
       because the superscript alef does not survive a shell paste.

    After all three: mean worst-token 0.9994, 0 of 60 ayahs below 0.5.

    DIACRITICS ARE REQUIRED. Every harakat-stripped variant scored EXACTLY
    0.000. The model genuinely hears vowelling, which is what makes
    per-token diagnosis (kasra vs sukun) meaningful.

  reftext.py implements all three rules. Use it; do not re-derive them.

testset/
  2,000 labelled synthetic items from injector.py, in three tiers
  (structural / prosodic / segmental) plus a CONTROL class.

  THE ARTIFACT CONTROL is why these numbers are credible: 10% of items are
  audio cut and rejoined AT THE SAME POINT -- a splice seam, no error,
  labelled negative. If a model flags those it is detecting EDITS, not
  mispronunciation. Measured gap: -0.007. Passed three times.

  DO NOT REGENERATE testset/. It is the anchor that makes v3, v4 and v5
  comparable.


===============================================================================
4. v5: WHAT WAS TRIED, AND THE ONE THING THAT MATTERS
===============================================================================

THE IDEA
  v4 uses a 72.6M general ASR model for a task where the verse is known in
  advance and Juz Amma has only ~2,200 distinct words. That is verification,
  not recognition -- the model never has to search a vocabulary, only check
  a hypothesis. So: distil a small model from the v4 teacher.

WHAT WAS BUILT
  teacher.py    generates supervision. For each ayah it builds augmented
                and/or deliberately corrupted variants, runs the v4 teacher
                over each, and records the per-word probability. Variants
                are stored as RECIPES (source + seed + operations), not
                audio -- render() regenerates them deterministically, so the
                audio the student sees is bit-identical to what the teacher
                scored.
  student.py    the small model, 811K params: depthwise-separable conv
                acoustic encoder + BiLSTM text encoder over CHARACTERS WITH
                HARAKAT + CTC head + cosine-similarity embedding head.
                Trained on distillation + in-batch metric learning + CTC.

THE OFFLINE RESULT -- THIS IS SOLID
      architecture                     params   holdout AUC   balanced
      BCE on a scalar, width 256        1.0M        0.880       82.0%
      CTC + metric + distillation       811K        0.919       86.7%

  Both heads combined at lambda 2.0. Held-out reciters. The CTC head ALONE
  (84.9%) beats the embedding head (82.7%), and the combination beats both.

  Distillation ablation: worth ~1.5pp balanced accuracy and ~0.014 AUC, plus
  CALIBRATION. Without it the model's outputs compress into a narrow high
  band -- ordering intact, scale useless.

THE STREAMING ATTEMPT -- THIS IS WHERE IT STANDS, AND IT DOES NOT WORK
  Five decoders were written against the trained student. All failed:

  1. SLIDING WINDOW + argmax over candidate end positions.
     The score climbed MONOTONICALLY with window length (0.53, 0.75, 0.79,
     0.86, 0.894) and never turned over, so argmax returned the longest
     candidate. The deliberate س->ص error scored 0.894, the HIGHEST score in
     its recording.

  2. CTC Viterbi, scored by MEAN log-probability.
     Confirmed EVERYTHING at every threshold from -1 to -5. The mean dilutes
     a single bad phoneme: ten characters at -0.1 and one at -5.0 averages
     to -0.55, barely distinguishable from a perfect -0.09.

  3. CTC Viterbi, scored by WORST CHARACTER.
     Ordering was right (correct -9.4 vs error -13.1) but calibration on
     reference audio showed correct words spanning -2.4 to -14.2, so no
     threshold separates. And Recording_4 -- a COMPLETELY WRONG VERSE --
     scored -8.9/-7.7/-8.4, BETTER than most correct words.

  4. KEYWORD-FILLER LIKELIHOOD RATIO
     score = logP(expected chars) - logP(best free path), per frame. This
     fixed the SCALE beautifully -- reference reciter median -0.096, p05
     -0.772 -- but still did not separate: Recording_4 scored -0.18/-0.27/
     -0.25, better than several correct recordings.

  5. DURATION CAP from the manifest, on top of (4). Untested at handover.

  THE DIAGNOSTIC OBSERVATION, and it explains all five:

      Word 0 of Recording_5 aligned over 0.15-3.90s -- 3.75 SECONDS for one
      word in a 6.23s recording, swallowing the next two words. The
      constrained path parks in BLANK states, and blank is usually the
      free-path winner too, so the constrained and filler scores rise by the
      SAME amount and the ratio is unchanged. Extending across arbitrary
      audio was FREE.

*** THE ROOT CAUSE ***
  THE STUDENT WAS TRAINED AS A VERIFIER AND USED AS A DETECTOR.

  Every training window was a correct crop of exactly one word, cut at true
  boundaries from the manifest, with ~150ms of context. The model was NEVER
  shown:
      - a window that does NOT contain the target word
      - a window where the word is at an unknown offset
      - a window containing part of a word, or two words
      - anything it should reject

  The CTC head in particular was an AUXILIARY LOSS on positives only. It
  learned to emit the right characters GIVEN a correct crop. It never
  learned to place a boundary or to reject.

  So it cannot say NOT FOUND, and five decoder rewrites were attempts to
  extract a capability the training never put there. Each fixed one symptom
  and exposed the next.


===============================================================================
5. HOW THE MODEL SHOULD BE TRAINED FOR STREAMING
===============================================================================

This is the actual task for whoever picks this up. The architecture,
corpus, teacher, recipes and evaluation harness all carry over. THE
TRAINING DATA AND OBJECTIVE ARE WHAT MUST CHANGE.

WHAT THE TRAINING SET NEEDS THAT IT DOES NOT HAVE

  NEGATIVE WINDOWS -- windows that do not contain the target word at all.
      Currently EVERY training window contains its own word. That single
      fact is why the model cannot say "not here". Sources: a different
      word's span, a span straddling a boundary, silence, another verse.

  VARIABLE OFFSET AND CROP -- the word at different positions within the
      window, with different amounts of leading and trailing context, and
      sometimes only partially present. At inference the window START is the
      cursor (roughly right) but the END is unknown and growing, so the
      model must handle a target that is halfway through arriving.

  A BACKGROUND / FILLER CLASS -- so "none of the expected words" is
      something the model can OUTPUT, rather than something the decoder
      approximates afterwards. This is the classical keyword-spotting
      answer and it was being reconstructed at decode time in attempt (4).

  BOUNDARY SUPERVISION -- so the model reports where the word ENDS rather
      than the decoder searching for it. The manifest has true boundaries
      for all 18,144 segments. A per-frame "is the word complete here"
      target is the natural form.

  DURATION AS A FEATURE, NOT A PATCH -- eight reciters have said every one
      of these words, so the plausible duration range is known per word.
      That belongs in training, not bolted on as a decode-time cap.

THE OBJECTIVE
  Frame-synchronous detection, not window classification. Per frame, the
  model should emit something like:
      p(the expected word has just completed here)
  trained with positives at true word-end frames and negatives everywhere
  else, plus the filler class. That is directly what the streaming loop
  needs and it removes the search entirely.

  CTC alignment can stay as an auxiliary loss -- it is genuinely useful for
  boundaries -- but it MUST see negatives, or it repeats the current
  failure.

WHAT TO KEEP
  - the distillation from v4's teacher. Six reciters cannot teach speaker
    invariance from scratch (v4a proved that); the teacher learned it from
    thousands of Tarteel voices. Distillation is how it transfers, and the
    ablation shows it also supplies calibration that relative objectives
    cannot.
  - the recipe system in teacher.py. Deterministic regeneration means the
    audio the student trains on is bit-identical to what the teacher scored.
  - the artifact control from injector.py. Any new evaluation must report
    the control false-positive rate FIRST.
  - Husary and Minshawy held out.
  - testset/ unchanged.

THE LITERATURE TO START FROM
  Jin et al., Interspeech 2024, "CTC-aligned Audio-Text Embedding for
  Streaming Open-vocabulary Keyword Spotting". Their acoustic encoder is
  155K PARAMETERS and competitive -- capacity is not the constraint here,
  and the current 811K model is already oversized. They train with CTC plus
  a multi-view metric loss, use in-batch negatives at BATCH 1024, compare by
  COSINE SIMILARITY rather than a learned head, and pool at WORD level (their
  ablation found character-level pooling the weakest of three).

  Also relevant: keyword-filler models (the classical way to say "not
  found"), and duration-penalised scoring (penalise a unit held longer than
  expected -- which is exactly the 3.75s failure above).


===============================================================================
6. HARD-WON DETAILS THAT ARE NOT IN ANY PAPER
===============================================================================

Each of these was the difference between working and not working.

  THE FORCED TASK PREFIX. Whisper's decoder always begins with
  <|startoftranscript|><|ar|><|transcribe|><|notimestamps|>. Omitting it
  makes the FIRST real token collapse for everyone -- a professional reciter
  scored logP -15 on a word he recites perfectly.

  WORST TOKEN, NOT MEAN. One wrong diacritic in an eight-token word barely
  moves the mean but IS the error. Measured: سُبَاتًا correct 0.994 worst,
  deliberate ص 0.000 worst. This lesson was learned in v4, WRITTEN DOWN, and
  then ignored in v5's streaming attempt 2. Do not repeat it.

  SILENCE TRIMMING. A pause before the learner starts gets absorbed into the
  FIRST word's span. Measured: a 0.6s lead-in took a word from z=4 to z=16.
  Without trimming, the embedding score for a correct recitation never
  exceeded 0.091; with it, 0.875.

  CLIPPED ONSETS. A learner who starts reciting before the recorder catches
  up loses the first consonant, and the device flags a word they said
  perfectly. Confirmed as the cause of one false flag.

  zero_infinity=True IN CTC IS CORRECT FOR TRAINING AND WRONG FOR SCORING.
  It turns an IMPOSSIBLE alignment (input shorter than the target) into a
  loss of ZERO -- which, scored as -loss/len, is the BEST POSSIBLE SCORE. A
  120ms window scored -0.00 and won the search.

  A RANDOM MODEL MUST FAIL YOUR METRIC. An UNTRAINED head scored 0.993 on a
  device-localization metric built in v4. Test this before trusting any
  number.

  ROWS MUST DIVIDE EVENLY. Eight reciters saying identical text cannot
  produce a fractional row count. Caught two silent parse bugs.

  SYNTHETIC TESTS KEPT PASSING WHILE REAL AUDIO FAILED. Every streaming
  rewrite passed its unit tests and then failed on the recordings. The
  recordings in recs/ are the only test that has been consistently
  informative. Run them early.


===============================================================================
7. METRICS -- READ THIS BEFORE TRUSTING A NUMBER
===============================================================================

Three metrics were each trusted too far in turn:

  corrupt<0.1      inflated by growing OVERCONFIDENCE. One checkpoint scored
                   62.6% with correlation 0.271; a BETTER one scored 32%
                   with 0.510. The rate was measuring confidence, not
                   discrimination.
  device top-1     an UNTRAINED model scored 0.993 on it, because the metric
                   asked which of ~3 words in an ayah this is -- easy, and
                   not what the device needs.
  Pearson r        penalises a model whose ranking is fine but whose outputs
                   are COMPRESSED. It made the no-distillation ablation look
                   0.10 worse when it was only ~1.5pp worse on balanced
                   accuracy.

USE AUC for any model-vs-model comparison: rank-based, so threshold-free AND
calibration-free. Use a THRESHOLD SWEEP (balanced accuracy at each cut
point) when choosing an operating point. Report the CONTROL false-positive
rate first, always.

And: cross-check any headline number against a metric that fails
differently, before believing it.


===============================================================================
8. WHAT IS NOT TRUE YET
===============================================================================

  ONE SPEAKER. Every uncontaminated result rests on one adult male voice.
  One speaker is an anecdote.

  CONTAMINATION. Tarteel fine-tuned on EveryAyah, the same corpus as this
  reference library, so the reference reciters are likely IN the teacher's
  training data. Held-out-reciter numbers are an upper bound.

  NO REAL LEARNER ERRORS. The synthetic set's artifact control proves the
  system is not detecting splice seams. It cannot prove the error taxonomy
  matches what students actually do. QDAT (1500+ voices, correct and
  incorrect recitation of three tajweed rules) is public and would help.

  NO TEACHER HAS SEEN IT. Nobody has asked a Kaduna teacher what students
  get wrong most, or whether the flags are pedagogically right.

  NO HAUSA-ACCENTED DATA. The target users are Hausa speakers. Whether the
  model handles that accent is completely unmeasured.

  PI NUMBERS ARE ESTIMATED at 4-5x measured laptop CPU time, not measured on
  hardware.

  TWO LICENCES OPEN in ATTRIBUTION.txt: everyayah audio redistribution, and
  Tanzil text conditions. Both must be resolved before any field deployment.

  TRAIN/VAL OVERLAP in v5: both splits were drawn from the same pool of
  ayah-reciter pairs with replacement, so ~49% of pairs appear in BOTH. Val
  numbers are inflated; holdout is clean. Partition the key list BEFORE
  drawing when regenerating.
