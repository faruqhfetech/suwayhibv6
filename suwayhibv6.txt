SUWAYHIB v6 -- PAPER DRAFT SKELETON
An offline, streaming, no-diagnosis pronunciation companion for Qur'anic
recitation.
Draft skeleton, written for continuation into a full ArabicNLP-style
submission. Every number below is either measured in this project or
cited from a named external source; nothing is invented, and places
where a result is designed-but-not-yet-executed are marked PENDING

===============================================================================
ABSTRACT (draft)
===============================================================================

We present Suwayhib, an offline, streaming system that gives learners of
Qur'anic recitation real-time feedback without ever naming the specific
error. Words on screen turn green as they are confirmed spoken; if the
green edge stops advancing, the learner repeats the last word. The
system never displays a wrong-word indication of any kind. This design
choice, made on pedagogical grounds (information without accusation, for
a child being told they got the Qur'an wrong), turns out to sidestep an
open problem the mispronunciation-detection field has itself named: the
lack of a reliable mapping from phoneme-level model output back to
actionable character-level feedback.

Suwayhib's v6 iteration reframes streaming recitation feedback as
constrained forced alignment with a rejection option, rather than
open-vocabulary keyword spotting -- exploiting that the verse, its word
order, and its phone sequence are all known in advance. We build a
frozen self-supervised encoder (wav2vec2-XLS-R-300M), a small trained
phone-recognition head (2.15M parameters), and a streaming decoder whose
design went through five documented failure iterations -- three of them
in this project alone, after five earlier ones in a prior version of the
system -- before literature grounding (Hwang et al., 2015) resolved the
central design question: a properly-trained CTC head needs no explicit
competing "filler" hypothesis at all.

We report conditioned word-verification F1 of 0.926 (in-distribution)
and 0.923-0.933 (held-out reciters) on a 2,000-item synthetic benchmark
with an artifact control, alongside a blind phoneme-recognition F1 of
0.41-0.41 on the IQRA 2026 shared-task leaderboard -- a harder,
differently-defined task that our system was not designed to optimise
directly, included for external comparability. We give a full account of
what did not work and why, since several of the negative results
(a keyword-filler tie that cannot be broken by construction; an
attrition failure mode that grows worse the longer a wrong hypothesis
survives) are, to our knowledge, not documented elsewhere in the
streaming-CTC literature in this form.


===============================================================================
1. INTRODUCTION
===============================================================================

1.1 The problem

Learning to recite the Qur'an correctly needs a teacher's ear: someone to
catch a mispronounced word, a skipped elongation, a merged sound. Between
lessons, learners practise alone, and small mistakes become habits. This
is sharpest in under-resourced settings -- Qur'an schools in Kaduna,
Nigeria, among them -- where teacher time is scarce and students practise
independently by default, not by choice.

Existing computer-assisted pronunciation tools for Arabic need an
internet connection and send the learner's recitation to a cloud server.
That is a real barrier where connectivity is unreliable, and it is also
a discomfort in its own right: families may reasonably prefer that a
private religious practice never leave the room.

1.2 What we build

Suwayhib is a small, low-cost, standalone practice companion that runs
entirely offline. The learner selects a starting verse, presses start
once, and recites continuously -- no button between words. Words on
screen confirm (turn green) as they are spoken. Confirmation is
MONOTONIC and never retracted: a word that goes green and then un-greens
is worse, we argue, than no feedback at all. If the green edge stops
advancing, the learner has hit something -- they can keep reciting or tap
any word to reset the cursor there. The session ends on a stop press or
a few seconds of silence.

Critically, THE SYSTEM NEVER MARKS A WORD WRONG. A mispronunciation is
communicated only as an absence of forward progress, never as a flagged
error, never in a colour that reads as a verdict. This is not withheld
information for its own sake; it is a considered choice, carried from
earlier iterations of this project, that a false stall costs a learner
one repetition, while a system that tells a child they got the Qur'an
wrong -- rightly or wrongly -- costs something we are not willing to
risk. Section 4.5 argues that this choice, made independently of the
literature, turns out to align with what the field's own most recent
shared task names as an unresolved, high-priority problem.

1.3 What "v6" means

This project has gone through five prior architectural iterations
(summarised in Section 2), each abandoned for a documented, specific
reason. v6 is a deliberate reframing of the task itself: not "does this
audio contain this word" (open-vocabulary keyword spotting, which has no
principled notion of absence), but "align this audio against a KNOWN
expected phone sequence and decide, frame by frame, whether the
alignment has completed" -- streaming forced alignment with rejection.
That reframing, and the negative results that led to it and away from it
again mid-implementation, are the paper's main technical content.


===============================================================================
2. RELATED WORK AND PROJECT HISTORY
===============================================================================

2.1 Prior iterations of this system (v1-v5)

  v1  MFCC/DTW features + classifier shopping. 0.82 AUC, F1~60 across
      three model families with different inductive biases, all
      plateauing at the same point -- the signature of a REPRESENTATION
      limit, not a model limit.

  v3  Duration-deviation scoring, no training. DTW-aligns the learner
      against reference reciters, scores word duration deviation from
      reference-reciter consensus. AUPRC 0.901 (holdout 0.915), artifact
      -control gap -0.007. Strong on gross timing errors (truncation
      0.944), blind to segmental content: cannot see a wrong phoneme of
      the right length.

  v4a Contrastive acoustic word embeddings. val AP 0.54 (unseen words,
      SEEN voices) but holdout AP 0.064 (unseen VOICES) -- the model
      learned WORDS, not speaker invariance, because speaker-invariance
      training in the literature uses hundreds to thousands of speakers
      and this corpus has six. With six voices, voice IS a reliable cue,
      and no architecture change fixes a data-scale limit. This single
      finding is the reason v6's phone head is built on a FROZEN,
      externally-pretrained self-supervised encoder rather than a
      trained-from-scratch one (Section 4.1).

  v4b Forced decoding with a fine-tuned Whisper (Tarteel-ai
      whisper-base-ar-quran, 72.6M params). Teacher-forces the decoder
      with the KNOWN verse text and reads the token-level probability
      assigned to each token. Reference reciters score worst-token
      median 1.0000 (p05 0.9993); a deliberate substitution scores
      0.000. SHIPPED, and it is the origin of the "worst token, not
      mean" lesson (a single bad token barely moves a MEAN score but IS
      the error) that recurs, ignored and then re-learned, twice more in
      v5 and once more in v6 (Section 5.2). Limitation: record-then-
      submit, not streaming.

  v5  Distillation toward a small (811K parameter) streaming student,
      trained via distillation + in-batch metric learning + CTC. Offline
      result solid (holdout AUC 0.919). FIVE STREAMING DECODERS WERE
      WRITTEN AND ALL FAILED. Root cause, diagnosed in the v5-to-v6
      handover: the student was trained as a VERIFIER (every training
      window a correct, pre-cropped span of exactly one word) and used
      as a DETECTOR (must decide, from a growing, unbounded window,
      whether the word is present at all). It was never shown a negative
      window, a partial window, or anything it should reject. No decoder
      rewrite can extract a capability the training never put there.

2.2 The IQRA / Iqra'Eval shared tasks

Iqra'Eval (ArabicNLP 2025) [El Kheir et al., 2025,
aclanthology.org/2025.arabicnlp-sharedtasks.61] introduced the first open
benchmark for Mispronunciation Detection and Diagnosis (MDD) in Qur'anic
recitation: given an utterance and its reference vowelised transcript,
predict the pronounced phoneme sequence, scored against both the
canonical sequence and a human-annotated verbatim sequence. IQRA 2026
[arXiv:2603.29087] expanded the corpus (Section 2.3) and reports a
leaderboard: whu-iasp (1st, F1 0.7201: frozen wav2vec2-xls-r-300m,
learnable multi-layer weighted fusion, a Temporal Convolutional Network,
CTC, two-stage curriculum, multi-checkpoint fusion and n-gram rescoring
at inference), UTokyo (2nd, F1 0.7170: Optimal Temporal Transport
Classification in place of plain CTC, addressing CTC's frame-alignment
limitations directly), and RAM (3rd, F1 0.7157: no architectural novelty
at all -- pure data curation via agreement-filtering across three
independently trained models, within 0.005 F1 of first place). The
closeness of these three, achieved by fundamentally different means, is
the strongest available evidence that for this task DATA QUALITY
DOMINATES ARCHITECTURE. The published baseline is F1 0.4414 (precision
0.3039, recall 0.7707).

A pattern the organisers report, and that recurs independently in this
project's own decoder history (Section 5.2): systems performing BELOW
baseline show high recall at very low precision, read as a system that
has not learned to discriminate and defaults to near-universal
rejection. Our own decoder failures showed the mirror image -- near-
universal ACCEPTANCE -- which is the same underlying deficit (a model or
decoder that cannot discriminate falls back on a constant answer) with
the sign flipped by which way the specific failure happened to lean.

The organisers name, as a stated future priority, evaluation frameworks
bridging phoneme-level model output to actionable character-level
feedback for learners, citing the lack of a reliable mapping from a
predicted erroneous phoneme back to the corresponding Arabic character
as one of the most pressing and underexplored open problems in Arabic
CAPT. Suwayhib's no-diagnosis interface (Section 1.2, Section 4.5) was
not designed with this problem in mind; it turns out to route around it
entirely, since the system never attempts the mapping at all.

2.3 Corpora

IqraEval/Iqra_train (~79h train, ~3.4h dev; native MSA speech, pseudo-
labelled -- speakers assumed correct, so phoneme_ref and phoneme_mis are
identical by construction). IqraEval/Iqra_TTS (~80h; synthetic speech
with DELIBERATELY introduced text-level errors before synthesis, so
phoneme_ref, the canonical sequence, and phoneme_mis, the sequence
corresponding to the synthesised error, genuinely differ).
IqraEval/Iqra_Extra_IS26 (~2h; REAL recordings of human mispronunciation,
collected specifically for IQRA 2026 -- gated access, requested during
this project, approval PENDING at time of writing). IqraEval/QuranMB.v2
(the evaluation set; audio and item ID only -- ground truth is
DELIBERATELY WITHHELD, confirmed by tracing the official scoring
repository, which ships alignment/scoring code but no truth file, plus
the existence of a Hugging-Face-Space leaderboard that scores submitted
predictions server-side).

2.4 CTC keyword spotting and the filler question

Hwang et al. [arXiv:1512.08903], "Online Keyword Spotting with a
Character-Level Recurrent Neural Network" -- CTC, character-level,
streaming, i.e. our exact setting -- report that a decoding network WITH
an explicit filler path and one WITHOUT (thresholding the keyword path's
own posterior directly) give "virtually no performance difference": the
filler network is not needed for a CTC keyword spotter, because CTC's
own blank symbol already absorbs the non-keyword modelling role filler
plays in classical keyword/filler HMM systems. Where filler-style
decoding IS used successfully alongside CTC, it is implemented as a
SEPARATE decoder on a separate graph, not as one additional state folded
into the keyword lattice [Rosenberg et al., arXiv:1710.09617]. This
literature was consulted only after three independent, increasingly
careful attempts at an in-lattice filler state had each failed for a
different, specific reason (Section 5.2) -- a sequence worth reporting
in full, since the failure modes are instructive independent of the
eventual (simpler) resolution.


===============================================================================
3. TASK DEFINITION
===============================================================================

Given: a known target verse, hence a known, ordered sequence of expected
words, hence (via a grapheme-to-phoneme module) a known phone sequence
per word. Given: a stream of audio frames arriving in real time. Decide,
strictly sequentially and monotonically: has the current expected word
now been spoken? If yes, advance the cursor and never revisit the
decision. If evidence stops supporting the current word, stall, without
indicating why.

This is DELIBERATELY NOT the IQRA/Iqra'Eval task (open-vocabulary or at
least verse-agnostic phoneme recognition, scored against a possibly-
erroneous verbatim reference, offline). It is closer to constrained
forced alignment with a rejection option. The distinction matters
because it changes what "good" performance looks like: the operating
point should be chosen to minimise FALSE CONFIRMS specifically, not
balanced accuracy or F1, for a reason argued in Section 4.4.


===============================================================================
4. METHOD
===============================================================================

4.1 Why a frozen encoder

v4a's finding (Section 2.1) is architectural: six voices cannot teach
speaker invariance from scratch, because with six voices, voice IS a
reliable predictive cue and any sufficiently expressive model will use
it. wav2vec2-XLS-R-300M [Babu et al.] is pretrained on approximately 436k
hours across 128 languages and already has the invariance our corpus
cannot teach. It is used FROZEN throughout -- no fine-tuning -- both for
this reason and because our compute budget (a single 4GB consumer GPU
for iteration, Kaggle sessions for larger runs) cannot support fine-
tuning a 300M-parameter model in any case.

4.2 Layer selection

A linear phone-classification probe was run over encoder layers 10-22
(XLS-R-300M has 24 transformer layers; the phonetic-information peak in
such models is reported in the 15-19 range, degrading toward both
extremes). Measured: majority-class baseline 0.2095; peak at layer 18
(0.3821); a smooth, single-peaked profile with spread 0.0308 across the
probed range (above a pre-registered flat-curve threshold of 0.02,
confirming the choice of encoder layer is not arbitrary). Layers 14-19
were selected as the band fed to the trained head, via a LEARNED
weighted sum (SUPERB-style) rather than a single fixed layer -- consistent
with, and independently corroborated by, the IQRA 2026 baseline's own
architecture choice.

4.3 The phone head

Cached XLS-R features (6 layers, 1024-dim, 50Hz) feed: a learned
softmax-weighted layer fusion; a linear input projection; 8 Conformer-
style blocks (depthwise-separable convolution restricted to chunk
boundaries -- "dynamic chunk convolution", so a single model serves any
streaming latency budget chosen at inference, following the U2/U2++
line of work -- plus chunked self-attention); a linear CTC output over
a 68-symbol phone inventory plus blank; and a SECOND, auxiliary output
head predicting per-frame word-END probability, supervised only where
verified word boundaries exist (our own reference-library manifest;
absent for streamed external corpora). 2.15M parameters at the
configuration used for the reported results (dim 128, 8 blocks,
feed-forward multiplier 2).

THERE IS NO TEXT ENCODER. The expected word's phone sequence enters the
DECODER as symbols, resolved directly against the head's output
distribution -- not as a learned embedding compared by similarity. This
deletion (relative to v5's design, which spent a large parameter share
on exactly such a text encoder) is a direct consequence of the v4a
finding: a learned audio-text similarity space is precisely the
mechanism that collapsed to speaker identity with six voices.

4.4 The streaming decoder

For the currently expected word's phone sequence p_1..p_N, a standard
CTC forced-alignment lattice is built: states
[blank, p_1, blank, p_2, ..., p_N, blank], with self-loops, forward
transitions, and a "skip the intervening blank" transition between two
DIFFERENT consecutive phones (refused between identical phones, to avoid
collapsing a genuine geminate). Scoring is Viterbi (max), in log space.

CONFIRMATION RULE. The word is confirmed once the length-normalised
log-posterior of the best completed alignment -- normalised by frames
the path has spent OCCUPYING A PHONE, not by wall-clock frames elapsed
-- exceeds a fixed threshold tau for several consecutive frames (a
"commit lag", absorbing single-frame spikes). No competing "filler"
hypothesis is modelled at all (Section 2.4, Section 5.2).

DURATION CAP. Each lattice state's self-loop is penalised once it has
been the winning transition for longer than a cap derived from measured
reference-reciter word durations; the penalty applies to the inherited
duration count for EVERY transition type into a state, not only literal
self-loops (Section 5.2 explains why the narrower version was
insufficient).

tau is chosen at a FIXED, LOW FALSE-CONFIRM RATE, not by balanced
accuracy. The argument: under monotonic, never-retracted confirmation, a
false stall costs the learner one repetition, but a false confirmation
silently advances the cursor past audio that was never verified, and
because confirmation cannot be retracted, everything downstream in that
recitation session is now misaligned. The two error types are not
interchangeable, and an operating-point criterion that treats them as
such (as balanced accuracy or F1 implicitly do) is measuring the wrong
thing for this product.

4.5 The no-diagnosis interface as a design choice, and its relation to
    an open problem in the field

The interface decision (Section 1.2) predates any contact with the IQRA
literature and was made on pedagogical grounds specific to this project's
population: young learners reciting scripture, where a computer telling
a child they are WRONG about the Qur'an carries a cost this project's
earlier iterations already treated as unacceptable, independent of
whether the flag is statistically justified. That the same design
choice also sidesteps a problem the field's most recent shared task
names explicitly as unresolved (Section 2.2) is presented here as a
notable alignment, not as evidence the design was informed by that
literature -- it was not, and the paper should be precise about that
distinction.

5. EXPERIMENTAL SETUP AND RESULTS
===============================================================================

5.1 Data pipeline validation (Gate 0 / Gate 1)

The reference-library manifest (18,144 verified word segments across 8
professional reciters, Juz Amma) was found to contain a real data-
integrity gap: a documented exclusion list (excluded_ayahs.json, 10
entries) had never actually been applied to the manifest it was meant to
filter, and separately undercounted a genuine timing defect found by
live cross-checking (Hani Rifai, 112:1, word overlap) that the project's
own verification report had already flagged but never propagated into
the exclusion file. Corrected manifest: 18,000 rows, 11 ayahs excluded,
all cross-checks (row-count-divides-evenly-by-reciter-count; word-count
agreement across reciters; span monotonicity) passing after correction.

A grapheme-to-phoneme module was built independently (not vendored from
the CC-BY-NC-licensed reference phonetiser the challenge inventory
derives from) and measured against the IQRA phoneme labels present in
Iqra_train: initial disagreement 21.5% across 2,000 sentences, reduced
to 1.5% after identifying and correcting: gemination represented as
symbol-doubling rather than a length feature (matching the challenge
convention); a systematic emphatic-vowel-allophone split the challenge
inventory retains despite its own paper reporting the distinction
collapsed for MSA; two orthographic-carrier edge cases (fathatan's
carrier alif; a shadda/vowel combining-mark ordering ambiguity); and one
missing base symbol (the consonant /l/, omitted from a hand-maintained
inventory set and later replaced by a corpus-measured inventory,
independently arriving at exactly the challenge's stated 68-phoneme
count once corrected).

5.2 The decoder's own negative results

Three filler-state designs were built and discarded for the trained
phone head's real output, in this order, before the literature (Section
2.4) was consulted:

  (i)   filler score = max(logp) per frame. By construction this is the
        best score ANY path can achieve that frame, so a PERFECT
        constrained match can only ever TIE it, never beat it. Verified
        directly: exit and filler scores converged to identical values
        to four decimal places on a clean synthetic test.

  (ii)  filler score = max(logp) minus a fixed per-frame penalty. Fixes
        (i), but the penalty is charged on silent frames too, where the
        constrained path correctly pays ~0. Given enough trailing
        silence after ANY word -- correct or not -- filler's fixed tax
        eventually exceeds any fixed constrained-path deficit,
        regardless of whether the word was ever validated. Reproduced
        directly: a deliberately wrong middle phone followed by 300 (and
        separately 1000) frames of silence confirmed anyway, with the
        constrained score sitting perfectly FLAT throughout -- zero new
        evidence, filler simply exhausted.

  (iii) filler score = logp[blank] (a silence/background model). Fixes
        (ii), but real CTC output is famously SPIKY -- blank-dominant on
        most frames even WITHIN a correctly recognised phone -- which
        the first two designs' clean-block synthetic tests never
        exercised. Under this design filler became nearly free almost
        everywhere, and essentially nothing confirmed, including
        plainly correct recordings.

Literature grounding (Hwang et al.) then established that no filler
state is required at all for a CTC-based streaming keyword spotter; the
decoder was rewritten to threshold the constrained path's own length-
normalised posterior directly (Section 4.4). Two further, independent
bugs were found and fixed during this rewrite, both by constructing
adversarial synthetic inputs before trusting any result on real audio:
an illegitimate lattice transition that let a word's completion appear
supported after a single frame, by inheriting a PRE-EVIDENCE baseline
score through the standard CTC dual-start convention; and a duration-cap
implementation that penalised only literal self-loops, missing an
exploit where a "skip" transition could re-derive fresh, uncapped
support from an unrelated, continuously-improving neighbouring phone
every single frame. The final decoder passes ten constructed regression
scenarios, including realistic spiky-CTC-shaped synthetic input and
silence-survival stress tests up to 100 seconds, and is the first
decoder in this project's history to correctly reject a genuine word
substitution in a real recording while confirming genuinely correct
recitation with zero false positives across the same small evaluation
set (Section 5.4).

5.3 Phone head: conditioned verification (v1 vs v2)

Two training configurations are reported. v1: standard 90/10 random
split over pooled training items (a methodological error, discovered
after training: Husary and Minshawy -- the project's designated held-out
reciters -- were present in the training set). v2: reciter-keyed split
(Husary/Minshawy strictly held out), boundary-loss weight raised
(0.3->1.0, targeting duration-sensitive error categories identified from
v1's per-category breakdown), SpecAugment added with a masking policy
that excludes time-masked frames from the boundary loss specifically (to
avoid teaching the boundary head from evidence just deleted), and early
stopping.

Evaluated on a 2,000-item synthetic benchmark (word-level flag detection
against seven injected corruption types plus a splice-seam artifact
control, held out by reciter into dev/holdout subsets):

              v1 (contaminated split)     v2 (reciter-held-out split)
  dev F1      0.9261                      0.9239
  holdout F1  0.9339                      0.9231
  artifact-control false-flag rate:
              17.0%                       30.0%   [REGRESSION]

Per-category pass rate (holdout+dev pooled, v1 -> v2):
  clean                    100.0% -> 100.0%
  control_seam             83.0%  -> 70.0%   [REGRESSION]
  madd_lengthening         89.0%  -> 96.5%   [improvement]
  madd_shortening          96.2%  -> 94.6%
  near_miss_substitution   99.0%  -> 98.5%
  truncation               100.0% -> 100.0%
  word_deletion            98.3%  -> 97.1%
  word_repetition          27.5%  -> 29.4%   [unresolved]
  word_substitution        97.5%  -> 98.0%

Two findings are read as substantive rather than noise. First, raising
the boundary-loss weight measurably helped exactly the duration-
sensitive category it targeted (madd_lengthening) while measurably
hurting artifact rejection (control_seam) -- plausibly the same
increased sensitivity to acoustic discontinuity cutting both ways,
though this is a hypothesis, not yet isolated by ablation. Second,
word_repetition did not move under either configuration, which is
better explained by a DATA gap than a loss-weight gap: neither training
source used (clean reference recitation; native, error-free read
speech) contains an example of word repetition, so no amount of
reweighting an existing loss term teaches a model a phenomenon it has
never once observed. Section 6 treats this as the most concrete,
actionable next step.

5.4 Streaming decoder, on the six recorded evaluation items

Six recordings, all from a native Hausa-speaking reciter (the target
population for this project, not a convenience proxy -- see the
correction to Section 6's limitations discussion), labelled from
listening into four categories: correct (2), weak articulation -- right
word, under-enunciated (1), deliberate substitution error (2), and one
capture artifact (clipped onset from a late-starting recorder,
distinguished from a genuine pronunciation error because the word was
said correctly and only the RECORDING is defective).

With the v2 checkpoint and the final (no-filler) decoder: both correct
recordings confirm fully; the artifact recording confirms fully (the
intended behaviour -- a defective capture should not stall a correctly
spoken word); the deliberate-substitution recording stalls EXACTLY at
the corrupted word, with the winning path's duration-cap counter far
below its cap (169 of 500 frames), confirming the rejection is driven by
genuine acoustic mismatch rather than a timing artifact. The two weak-
articulation/near-miss recordings stall one word earlier than their
labelled target, at a word independently confirmed (via the v4b Whisper
scorer) to be genuinely under-articulated in that recording -- correct
per the "stall on weak evidence" design intent, though it means these
two items do not exercise detection of the SPECIFIC labelled error
further downstream in the utterance.

A tau-sweep across the 2,000-item benchmark's holdout split (Section
5.3's reciters) found NO single threshold jointly maximising both
correct-recording completion and error-recording rejection: the best
joint operating point (tau = -1.40) gives 97.5% correct-completion
against 89.2% error-rejection: an operating point, not a solved problem.
Per-category behaviour at this operating point mirrors Section 5.3's
finding closely: every segmental category (word_substitution,
word_deletion, near_miss_substitution, truncation, control_seam) at or
near ceiling; every duration/repetition category
(word_repetition 72.0%, madd_lengthening 74.1%, madd_shortening 89.3%)
markedly weaker. This is read as confirmation that the decoder is
correctly exposing a PHONE HEAD limitation rather than masking or
compensating for one -- no threshold choice closes a gap the underlying
posteriors do not support.

5.5 Blind phoneme recognition (external leaderboard)

Suwayhib's phone head, run through an unconstrained greedy CTC decode
(no expected-verse conditioning at all -- a different, harder task the
system is not designed to optimise for; included for external
comparability only) and submitted to the IQRA 2026 leaderboard: v1
checkpoint F1 0.4087 (precision 0.2710, recall 0.8313); v2 checkpoint
approximately 0.41 (self-reported, exact figure not independently
re-derived at time of writing). Both sit below the published baseline
(0.4414) and in the lower portion of a 22-entry leaderboard topped at
0.7201. The precision/recall imbalance (recall well above precision at
both checkpoints) matches the organiser-reported pattern for below-
baseline systems (Section 2.2): evidence the model under-discriminates
on this harder, unconditioned task specifically, consistent with
Section 5.3's finding that the SAME checkpoint reaches 0.92-0.93 F1 on
the conditioned verification task it was actually designed for.


===============================================================================
6. LIMITATIONS
===============================================================================

Evaluation, aside from the 2,000-item synthetic benchmark, rests on SIX
recordings from ONE speaker. That speaker is a native Hausa speaker,
which is the project's actual target population (not a generic proxy
voice, and not merely "one adult male voice" as an earlier internal
document characterised it) -- but n=1 remains n=1, and the path to more
speakers (family members and students in the same community) is
concrete, low-cost, and not yet executed.

No teacher has evaluated whether the system's stalls are pedagogically
correct -- i.e. whether stalling matches what a human teacher would
actually flag. This is arguably a larger open question than any metric
in Section 5.

The artifact-control false-flag rate (17-30% depending on
configuration) is well above a historical benchmark from an earlier
non-learned scoring method in this project (0.7%), and the v2 direction
of change on this metric was a regression. This is unresolved.

word_repetition detection is weak (27-29%) under both reported
configurations and is attributed to a training-data gap (no repetition
examples in the training corpora used), not a modelling gap; Section 7
proposes a specific, mechanistically-motivated fix.

The deployed model still runs the full 315M-parameter frozen encoder.
Distillation toward a browser/phone-deployable size (target
approximately 600K parameters, INT8, under 2MB) has not been attempted.
Every result in this paper is from a development-time checkpoint, not a
deployment-ready one.

A full retrain incorporating the corpus's verbatim-mispronunciation
labels (phoneme_mis, distinct from the canonical phoneme_ref this
project's earlier training used exclusively -- a distinction this paper
argues is the most likely explanation for the word_repetition gap) and
the two-stage curriculum the strongest external systems report (Section
2.2) has been designed and unit-tested (streaming data pipeline,
length-bucketed batching measured at roughly 32% compute savings versus
unbucketed batching, tri-stage learning-rate schedule, checkpoint/resume
for session-limited compute) but NOT YET EXECUTED to completion at time
of writing.

7. FUTURE WORK
===============================================================================

Train on phoneme_mis rather than phoneme_ref, unlocking the ~80h
Iqra_TTS corpus (currently unusable under a canonical-label training
objective, since it would teach the model to emit the CORRECT sequence
for audio containing a deliberate error). Adopt the two-stage curriculum
(breadth training on Iqra_train+Iqra_TTS, then adaptation on
Iqra_Extra_IS26's real human errors) that separates the top three IQRA
2026 leaderboard entries from lower-ranked ones by margin, not
architecture.

Isolate, via ablation, whether the control_seam regression (Section 5.3)
is genuinely caused by the raised boundary-loss weight, as hypothesised,
or is confounded with the same run's other simultaneous changes.

Re-run the tau sweep (Section 5.4) against a retrained checkpoint,
across a materially larger held-out speaker set.

Attempt encoder distillation toward a deployment-viable size, using the
already-frozen phone head as a fixed measuring instrument for the cost
of shrinking (a methodology this project used successfully once already,
to select the XLS-R layer band in Section 4.2).

Collect Hausa-accented recordings at the scale of tens of speakers
rather than one, and bring a Kaduna or comparable teacher's judgement to
bear on whether the system's stalls are pedagogically correct -- both
of which are, notably, work this paper's own field names as an open
research priority (Section 2.2), and neither of which requires further
modelling work to begin.
